# Data Sources

## The short version

Every package/exclusion record in `backend/internal/hbp/data/*.json` has a `verified` boolean and a `source_note` string. If you take one thing from this document: **check `verified` before you trust a number**, and read this file before removing that field from anywhere in the codebase.

As of this update, **300 of 315 package records are verified directly against government-published HBP rate schedules** — up from 274 of 289 after the previous session added two new real sources, 24 of 40 the session before that, and 1 the session before that. The other 15 are still seed/placeholder, clearly marked, for reasons explained below. This latest jump (19 August 2026) resolved the HBP 2.1-vs-2022 version currency question the previous session deliberately left open, and reached General Medicine records that session's extraction found but couldn't yet place — see "19 August session" below.

## What's actually verified

Three rounds of verification happened during this build (August 2026):

1. **PMJAY covers 1,949 procedures across 27 specialties** — confirmed via web search against current published figures.
2. **The "Unspecified Procedure" discretionary catch-all is capped at ₹1,00,000**, within the overall ₹5,00,000 per-family annual limit — confirmed directly against the National Health Authority's own HBP 2.2 User Guidelines. `backend/internal/hbp/data/hbp_packages.json`'s `UNSPECIFIED` entry, and the named constants `hbp.UnspecifiedProcedureCapINR` / `hbp.AnnualFamilyLimitINR` in `backend/internal/hbp/types.go`.
3. **24 package records checked directly against the NHA's own HBP 2.1 master package list** (`https://nha.gov.in/img/pmjay-files/HBP-2.1.pdf`), fetched and cross-checked during this build on 14 August 2026. This is the same official rate schedule an empanelled hospital's own billing system references — package code, name, specialty group, and total package price are now real government data for these 24 records, not estimates. Specifically:
   - **Two existing placeholders were corrected**, not just re-labeled: `SEED-CARD-001` (PTCA with single stent) had a placeholder rate of ₹65,000; the real rate for the equivalent package (MC011A, one bare-metal stent) is ₹49,300 — a real, meaningful gap between guess and government figure, which is exactly the kind of thing "verified: false" exists to flag. `SEED-NEPH-001` (Haemodialysis) had its `specialty` corrected from "Nephrology" to "General Medicine" — the real master list groups this procedure differently than the placeholder assumed, which matters because `specialty` is what the family-facing sentence checks the hospital's empanelment against.
   - **One vague placeholder was replaced with five real, distinct tiers.** `SEED-PAED-001` ("Neonatal Intensive Care, Per Day, Level II" — one guessed number) is gone; in its place are `MN001A`–`MN005A`, the real five-tier neonatal severity structure (₹500 / 3,000 / 5,000 / 6,000 / 7,000), each independently priced in the government schedule. This is a strict improvement, not just a correction — a family whose baby is at a specific, describable severity now matches a real tier instead of one averaged guess.
   - **Sixteen genuinely new real entries were added**: Burns Management (2), Emergency Medicine (3), additional Cardiology interventional procedures (7, beyond the pre-existing PTCA/CABG/angiogram entries), Mental Health/ECT (2), and two more Neonatal Care entries (ROP surgery, VP shunt). Every one carries `"verified": true` and a `source_note` citing the exact document and fetch date.

Everything else the source spec asserts (exclusion categories, the scale figures, CGRMS/NALSA mechanics) is carried forward from *that document's* own research log (its Section 22) — sourced there to a reported Rajya Sabha government reply and public NHA materials, but not independently re-verified from scratch by this build. Each exclusion record's `source_note` says exactly this, unchanged from before.

### Where real extraction hit a genuine wall

`web_fetch` against the master PDF stopped at the exact same point (mid-entry, partway through the Medical Oncology section) regardless of how high a `text_content_token_limit` was requested — including a request 5x larger than what actually got returned. That's a strong signal of a **server-side extraction cap** on this tool for a document this size, not a limit under this session's control. Practical effect: the document is ordered by two-letter specialty-group code (Burns, Emergency, Infectious Disease, Interventional Neuroradiology, Cardiology, General Medicine, Mental Disorders, Neonatal Care, Medical Oncology, in that order, at minimum), and extraction reached through the start of Medical Oncology before hitting that wall. Specialties that come later in the document — General Surgery, Orthopaedics, Obstetrics & Gynaecology, Ophthalmology, ENT, Urology, Neurosurgery among them, based on the specialty list PMJAY publishes — were **not reached**, including the exact procedures the pre-existing `SEED-GS-*`, `SEED-ORTHO-*`, `SEED-OBG-*`, `SEED-OPH-*`, `SEED-ENT-*`, `SEED-URO-*`, and `SEED-NEURO-*` placeholders represent. Those remain unverified, not because no one tried, but because this specific attempt had a real, hit ceiling. Whoever picks this up next should know that ceiling exists before re-attempting the same approach expecting a different result — a different extraction strategy (splitting the fetch by a different method, or a source that publishes per-specialty files rather than one combined PDF) is worth trying before assuming the data simply isn't gettable.

**Update, 15–16 August session: that "try a different source" suggestion was followed up on, and it worked — partially.** See the next section for what a genuinely different extraction strategy found, and for confirmation that the *specific* ceiling described above is real and reproducible across completely different documents and domains, not an artifact of that one PDF.

### Schema change: `rate_type`, `per_diem_rates`, `rate_max_inr`

The previous session's own "what was deliberately left out" section (below) correctly predicted this would need a schema change, not just a data-file edit. `backend/internal/hbp/types.go`'s `Package` struct gained three new, additive, `omitempty` fields — every one of the original 40 records has `rate_type` empty and renders exactly as before; nothing about the original citation logic changed for them.

- **`rate_type: "per_diem"`** — the package is reimbursed per day of admission, stratified by ward level, not as one total. `indicative_rate_inr` holds the Routine Ward figure; `per_diem_rates` holds the full breakdown (`routine_ward_inr` / `hdu_inr` / `icu_no_vent_inr` / `icu_vent_inr`). Confirmed uniform across nearly all 156 per-diem diagnoses added this session: **₹2,300 / ₹3,630 / ₹9,350 / ₹9,900** per day. `internal/response/templates.go`'s `packageCitation()` now renders this as an explicit four-level "per day" sentence — never a single number a family could read as a total. `TestPackageCitation_PerDiemNeverImpliesATotal` in `internal/response/templates_test.go` is the specific regression test for this property.
- **`rate_type: "tiered"`** — the true rate depends on the treating hospital's city-tier classification (used for the Cardiology/High-End/Oncology records from Source B). `indicative_rate_inr` holds the floor (Tier3/Z), `rate_max_inr` holds the ceiling (Tier1/X). `packageCitation()` renders this as an explicit range — deliberately never picks one tier to state as *the* number, since this tool has no way to know which tier a specific family's hospital actually falls under, and picking one would be a guess dressed up as a fact. `TestPackageCitation_TieredShowsRangeNotSingleNumber` covers this.
- Both new paths are validated at load time in `internal/hbp/loader.go` (`per_diem_rates` levels must strictly increase; a `tiered` record's max must genuinely exceed its floor) — a malformed record fails the build, not a family's screen. 12 new tests across `internal/hbp/loader_ratetype_test.go` and `internal/response/templates_test.go` cover this end to end.

### Why Medical Oncology is package-level, not regimen-level

Source B lists chemotherapy at *regimen* granularity — up to 16 different named drug regimens under a single package like "CT for CA Breast," each independently priced (Tamoxifen at the cheap end, a monoclonal antibody at the expensive end). Matching a family's description to one specific regimen and citing its exact rate would be its own kind of overclaiming: a family says "chemo for my mother's breast cancer," not a drug name, and stating one regimen's price as though it were confirmed could directly contradict what their oncologist is actually prescribing — undermining a true denial dispute rather than helping it. Each of the 39 entries added is therefore `rate_type: "tiered"`, using the cheapest-to-most-expensive regimen rate found in that package as the floor and ceiling, with the specific regimen names preserved in `confidence_notes` for a human reviewer rather than surfaced to a family as a specific match. `MO039` (Hodgkin's lymphoma) is explicitly flagged in its own `confidence_notes` as extracted right at Source B's wall — its regimen list may be incomplete even by this package-level standard.

### `SEED-ONCO-001` retired

The original unverified placeholder — "Chemotherapy, Per Cycle, Standard Protocol," one guessed number covering all cancer types — is now superseded by 39 specific, verified, real oncology packages and was removed. This was judged lower-risk than the rate-currency question below: it was already marked `"verified": false`, so removing an acknowledged placeholder carries none of the risk of silently rewriting a number a previous session had verified. Keeping it alongside 39 precise real entries would also have made retrieval strictly worse — a vague low-specificity placeholder competing against precise matches for the same query.

### RESOLVED, 19 August session: HBP 2.1 vs. HBP 2022 rate currency

The previous session found this pattern but stopped short of acting on it — see "What a future session should do with this" below for exactly what it asked for. This session did that:

**Version currency is confirmed, not circumstantial.** Three independent sources corroborate that HBP 2022 genuinely supersedes HBP 2.1, and even the intermediate HBP 2.2:

- An NHA-affiliated official document catalog (`snomedct.abdm.gov.in/hospital/hbc`) lists "National Master Health Benefit Packages 2.1," "User guidelines for HBP 2.2," "National Master Health Benefit packages 2.2," and "HBP 2022 package master and OM" as four separate, sequential publications.
- A PIB (Press Information Bureau) press release dated 6 October 2021 describes HBP 2.2 as a flat percentage-based rate revision (20–400% increases, ~400 procedures) rolling out November 2021 — no mention of city-tier differential pricing.
- Independent news coverage (Tribune India; a second, separate report of the same event) of NHA's own 7–8 April 2022 "Ayushman Sangam" launch event at Mahabalipuram, Tamil Nadu describes HBP 2022 as adding 365 new procedures (total 1,949) and introducing "differential pricing... for the first time" based on city type — the exact Tier1(X)/Tier2(Y)/Tier3(Z) structure Source B uses. This is the specific, checkable, structural fingerprint that distinguishes HBP 2022 from every version before it.

**Criteria stability was checked directly, not assumed.** The previous session's caution wasn't just about version sequencing — it was specifically that *criteria*, not just prices, might have shifted (the BM001B Burns Management TBSA-threshold example). This session re-fetched Source B directly — reaching Cardiology in full (see below for exactly how far this fetch got and where it stopped) — and confirmed all seven flagged Cardiology records are simple, single-criterion procedure packages in HBP 2022: no TBSA-style eligibility bands, no criteria text at all to compare against HBP 2.1. The criteria-shift risk that correctly blocked the previous session does not apply to these seven records.

**What changed:** `MC005A`, `MC007A`, `MC008A`, `MC009A`, `MC015A`, `MC016A`, `MC017A` are now `"rate_type": "tiered"`, each summing HBP 2022's procedure fee and separately-listed implant/device cost into one bundled total per city-tier (matching how the superseded HBP 2.1 figure was already structured — see each record's `confidence_notes`, which preserves the old flat figure rather than discarding it, per the previous session's own instruction for how to do this). See `backend/internal/hbp/data/reconcile_2022_rates.py` for the exact transcription, kept for the same audit-trail reason `transform.py` and `transform_2022_additions.py` were kept.

**A finding for whoever picks up the rest of the `nha.gov.in`-sourced portion of this dataset:** the previous session's hypothesis — "it's plausible the entire remaining `nha.gov.in`-sourced portion of this dataset has the same one-version staleness" — held for all seven records checked this session, no exceptions. Worth treating as the working assumption for the next reconciliation pass, not re-litigating from scratch each time.

## What's a seed/placeholder, and why (the remaining 15)

The seed dataset takes a specific, honest middle path for what real extraction hasn't reached yet:

- **Package names and specialties are real.** "Laparoscopic Cholecystectomy," "Caesarean Section (C-Section) Delivery," and so on are genuine, published PMJAY package categories — common medical/scheme terminology, not invented.

## Why the response templates are English-only

The extraction layer (`internal/extract`) understands Malayalam/English code-mixed *input* natively — that's the model's job, and it's genuinely one of the three reasons (source spec Section 8) this isn't just a keyword lookup. But the *output* — the actual sentences shown to a family in `internal/response/templates.go` — is hard-coded English.

This was a deliberate choice, not an oversight: machine-translating safety-critical, medical/legal-adjacent text (a wrong translation of "this is genuinely more complicated than a simple yes or no" around an organ-transplant exclusion, for instance) without a native-speaker review is a real way to cause harm, not a shortcut to take casually. Real Malayalam localization of these specific templates — reviewed by someone fluent, ideally someone who has actually had this exact conversation with a family — is honest future work, tracked here rather than silently assumed away.
