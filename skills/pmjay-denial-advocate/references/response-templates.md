# Response templates

Use these as scaffolding, not as text to paste blindly. Keep the care-first
line first, keep sentences short, and replace every `[...]` placeholder with
something the family actually told you. Never invent a detail.

## The care-first line

Say or write this before the outcome, every time:

> Get treatment first. Dispute the money after. Always. If you can pay now and
> settle the dispute later, or move to a different hospital, do that: do not
> let this disagreement delay or stop care.

## Green

> Get treatment first. Dispute the money after. Always. If you can pay now and
> settle the dispute later, or move to a different hospital, do that: do not
> let this disagreement delay or stop care.
>
> **What I understood:** [one sentence].
>
> **What this likely means:** This matches a package listed as covered under
> PMJAY: **[package name] ([specialty])**, listed at an indicative rate of
> **[₹rate]** ([tiered range / per-day breakdown, if applicable]). If this
> hospital is empanelled for [specialty], it should not be asking you to pay
> for this procedure as a private charge.
>
> **At the desk, say:** "This is [package name], HBP code [code], which is a
> covered PMJAY package. Please check the pre-authorisation under my card and
> give me the written reason if you are saying it is not covered."
>
> **If they refuse:** Ask for the written reason and the pre-authorisation
> status. Call **14555**. Keep the bill and the denial note. Do not delay care.
>
> **Keep:** card, admission papers, doctor's procedure note, itemised bill,
> the name and designation of the person who refused.
>
> This is guidance based on official PMJAY rules, not a legal ruling. Confirm
> the current rate with the hospital or 14555 before relying on any figure.

If the record is unverified, use this instead of a rate:

> This is a listed PMJAY procedure, but the exact package code and rate for it
> still need to be confirmed with the hospital or 14555.

## Amber — low confidence

> **What I understood:** [one sentence].
>
> **Why this is not a clear answer:** The closest match I can find is
> **[package name]**, but I am not confident enough to tell you it is
> definitely this. Guessing here could do more harm than good.
>
> **Ask this first:** "What is the exact procedure name the doctor has written,
> and what code is the hospital billing it under?"
>
> If you have the discharge summary or the doctor's note, read the procedure
> name from it. If you do not, call **14555** with the hospital name and the
> doctor's words.

## Amber — close candidates

> There is more than one PMJAY package this could genuinely be, and which one
> applies changes the answer. The closest is **[package A]**; another plausible
> one is **[package B]**. Do not let the desk treat them as the same thing.
>
> **Ask:** "Which procedure name and HBP code are you using for this bill?"
>
> If the desk cannot say, ask the doctor. If neither can, call **14555**.

## Amber — pending pre-authorisation

> This may be about **pre-authorisation**, which is not the same as a denial.
> Before anything else, ask the billing desk exactly this:
>
> "Has the pre-authorisation actually been submitted for this case, and what is
> its current status: approved, pending, or denied?"
>
> - If it is **pending**, ask for the submission reference and the expected
>   decision time. A pending case is not a refusal. Do not file a grievance yet.
> - If it is **denied**, ask for the written denial and the appeal route.
> - If the desk cannot tell you, call **14555** and ask them to check.
>
> Do not delay treatment while this is sorted out.

## Amber — partial exclusion

> This is genuinely more complicated than a yes or no. [Exclusion description]
> [Nuance.] Whether this specific case is covered depends on your state and the
> exact procedure. It is not a blanket rule either way.
>
> **Next step:** call **14555** or ask a NALSA Para Legal Volunteer on **15100**
> to check your state's rule. Do not pay a large amount or file a grievance
> until someone confirms the state-specific position.

## Amber — unspecified procedure

> I cannot find a named PMJAY package that fits well enough to say this is
> covered. That does not automatically mean it is excluded: PMJAY has a
> discretionary category for real procedures that do not map to a named
> package, but the hospital has to apply it.
>
> **Ask:** "What procedure name and code are you billing this under, and are
> you considering the Unspecified Procedure category?"
>
> If the answer is no, call **14555**.

## Red — the denial is correct

> I want to be straightforward with you rather than suggest there is a dispute
> to be had. [Exclusion description] This is a general rule of the scheme, not
> this hospital being difficult.
>
> As a [category], this is not something to file a grievance about, because the
> hospital is applying the rule correctly. If a doctor has documented a
> functional medical reason behind the procedure, ask them to put that in
> writing; that can change how it is classified.
>
> **Alternatives:** ask about a payment plan, a state scheme, or a hospital
> discount. Do not delay care you need.

## Mixed — two parts

> What you have described has two separate parts, and they should be handled
> separately, not accepted or rejected as one bill.
>
> **Covered part:** [package name] ([specialty]), listed at [rate]. If the
> hospital is empanelled for [specialty], this part should be cashless.
>
> **Not covered part:** [exclusion display name]. [Description.] This part is
> correctly not covered, regardless of the rest of the bill.
>
> **Ask the billing desk to split the bill into these two parts explicitly**,
> and dispute only the first part. Get the split in writing.
>
> If the desk refuses to split it, call **14555**.

## Handoff

> This sounds like it involves more than one thing at once, and like a lot to
> sort through alone right now. A Para Legal Volunteer's job is helping
> families through exactly this kind of situation, for free, and they are not
> employed by this hospital.
>
> Call **15100** and read them this summary:
>
> [Handoff summary: who the patient is, what happened, what the hospital said,
> what has been paid, what documents exist, what the family is most worried
> about, and what has already been tried.]

## Hospital script — short version

For a family reading aloud at the desk:

> "Please write down the package name and HBP code you are saying does not
> apply, and the reason. My card is active. If you are saying it is not
> covered, I need that in writing, with the pre-authorisation status. I am not
> refusing to pay anything I owe; I am asking you to check the package before I
> pay. Please also give me an itemised bill."

Keep it calm. Do not argue about motives. Ask for facts and paper.

## Complaint draft

```text
--- Draft complaint for CGRMS (the family submits it; this is not a submission) ---
Date prepared: [date]

Subject: [Denial of covered service / Demand for payment for a covered service /
Delay in pre-authorisation] — [procedure name]

Patient: [name, if the family wants to include it]
PMJAY card: [number, if the family wants to include it]
Hospital: [name, department]
Date and time of incident: [date, approximate time]

What happened:
[Two or three factual sentences in the family's own words. Quote the desk if
possible.]

What the hospital said:
["..." — exact words, or the closest recollection.]

Package the family believes applies:
[Package name and HBP code, if known. If unverified, say "package to be
confirmed" and do not state a rate.]

Written reason given by the hospital:
[Quote it, or state: "No written reason was given."]

Pre-authorisation status:
[Submitted / pending / approved / denied / not applicable / unknown, with the
reference number if available.]

Care affected:
[What was delayed, denied, or charged for. Do not exaggerate.]

What the family is asking for:
[Review of the denial / correction of the bill / refund / written explanation.]

Attachments:
[Itemised bill, payment receipts, denial note, discharge summary, evidence log.]

--- End of draft. Review every detail before submitting. ---
```

Every bracketed fact must come from the family. Do not fill in a card number, a
hospital name, or a date because it seems plausible.

## Evidence prompt

Ask the family to note, while it is fresh:

- Who said it (name and designation, if visible).
- The exact time and desk.
- The exact words used.
- Whether a written reason was offered; if not, ask for one.
- The pre-authorisation reference and status.
- What documents they have and what is still with the hospital.

## Handoff summary

A PLV should be able to pick this up cold. Include:

1. Patient and family context, only what is relevant.
2. The procedure and the medical reason.
3. What the hospital said and did.
4. What has been paid, and what receipts exist.
5. The pre-authorisation status.
6. The documents available.
7. The specific unresolved question.
8. What the family has already tried.
9. The family's preferred language and how to reach them.

## Disclaimer, every time

> This is guidance based on official PMJAY rules, not a legal ruling. Confirm
> the current rate and status with the hospital or the PMJAY helpline (14555)
> before relying on any figure. Free legal help from a NALSA Para Legal
> Volunteer is available on 15100.
