# Escalation, rights, and evidence

Use this file when the family needs a next step, not just an explanation.
Verify contact details before relying on them in an emergency; the routes below
are the standard published ones, but staffing and portals change.

## First, the care rule

Never delay or cancel treatment to pursue a billing dispute. If the hospital
is threatening to stop care, call **112** and **14555** immediately and ask for
the hospital's written reason. Payment disputes are resolved after care is
protected.

## Who to contact

| Route | Number / channel | What it is for |
|---|---|---|
| Emergency | **112** | Immediate danger, including a patient being detained or care being withdrawn. |
| PMJAY helpline | **14555** | Scheme coverage, hospital empanelment, pre-authorisation status, grievance guidance. |
| NALSA legal aid | **15100** | Free legal aid. Ask for a Para Legal Volunteer (PLV). |
| CGRMS | Ayushman app or the PM-JAY grievance portal | Formal grievance against a hospital or State Health Agency. The family files it. |
| State Health Agency (SHA) | Through 14555 or the state PMJAY site | State-specific package rules, empanelment, pre-auth escalations. |
| Hospital grievance desk | Ask at the hospital | First written complaint; get a receipt or reference number. |

The PMJAY Advocate skill cannot submit a CGRMS complaint, check a live
pre-authorisation, or connect the family directly to a PLV. It prepares the
text and the summary so the family does not have to explain everything again.

## What to ask the hospital for, in writing

Ask for these specifically. "The computer says no" is not a reason.

1. **The exact package name and HBP code** the hospital says applies.
2. **The written reason for refusal**, including the clause or exclusion
   category being relied on.
3. **The pre-authorisation reference number and status** — submitted, pending,
   approved, or denied. If denied, the written denial.
4. **The hospital's PMJAY empanelment status** and whether this specialty is
   empanelled.
5. **An itemised bill** separating covered treatment from non-covered items.
6. **The name and designation** of the person giving the refusal.
7. **A receipt** for every payment, and a copy of every form signed.

If the hospital will not give a written reason, note the time, the desk, and
the words used, then call 14555.

## Evidence checklist

Capture while it is still available. Do not put yourself in danger or block
care to take a photo.

- PMJAY card / Ayushman card, and the patient's ID.
- Admission papers, discharge summary, investigation reports.
- Doctor's procedure name, diagnosis, and any note saying the procedure is
  medically necessary.
- Itemised bill and payment receipts.
- Pre-authorisation reference and any SMS/portal status.
- The refusal: who said it, designation, date, time, exact words.
- Any written denial, form, or screenshot.
- Names of witnesses (other family members, other patients if appropriate).
- A short timeline: what happened, in order, with times.

Store copies, not originals. Never hand over the only original of a document.

## How to phrase the CGRMS complaint

Use facts, not adjectives. A useful structure:

1. Patient and card details (only what the family chooses to include).
2. Hospital name, department, date and time.
3. What the hospital said, quoted as closely as possible.
4. The package or procedure the family believes applies, with the code if
   known.
5. What written reason was given, or that none was given.
6. What care was delayed, denied, or charged for.
7. What the family is asking for: review, refund, correction of records, or
   disciplinary review.
8. Attachments: bill, denial, discharge summary, evidence log.

Do not claim a specific rupee amount is owed unless the family can support it
with a bill or a published rate. Do not allege fraud without evidence. Ask for
a review, not a punishment.

## Detention, threats, and coercion

If a hospital refuses to release a patient, withholds a body, threatens the
family, or demands payment before allowing discharge:

- Call **112**.
- Call **14555** and report the hospital.
- Contact **NALSA 15100** for free legal help.
- Ask for the hospital's written policy and the name of the person in charge.
- Do not sign blank documents or "undertakings" under pressure.
- Do not hand over original identity documents as security.

This is beyond a coverage dispute. Say so plainly and route to Handoff.

## What a family should not do

- Do not delay emergency care to argue about money.
- Do not refuse treatment that is already underway.
- Do not pay cash without a receipt.
- Do not sign a blank or unexplained form.
- Do not accept a verbal "not covered" as final without asking for the written
  reason.
- Do not let a hospital keep original documents as collateral.
- Do not file a grievance alleging fraud without evidence.
- Do not assume a pending pre-authorisation is a denial.

## If the family cannot read or write

Offer to read the script aloud, keep it short, and use the helpline. Do not
require a smartphone, an app, or an email address to take the next step. A
phone call to 14555 is a valid next step on its own.

## Disclaimer to repeat

> This is guidance based on official PMJAY rules, not a legal ruling. Confirm
> the current rate and status with the hospital or 14555 before relying on any
> figure. Free legal help from a NALSA Para Legal Volunteer is available on
> 15100.
